When Your System Fails in Raleigh

Here's what we see most often when something breaks at home: warm air from the AC, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that ignites and shuts off, a breaker that trips every time the system kicks on, an evaporator coil that has iced over, a condensate drain overflowing into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has quit. Two common situations call for a quick step from you before our technician gets there. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: switch the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because continuing to run it can flood the drain pan and hurt the compressor. The second is a furnace that short-cycles or produces a burning smell. Shut it down at the switch and give us a call. That small step can save you from a much larger repair.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Raleigh

A little routine care makes all the difference between a system that lasts and one that quits in the middle of a heat wave. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, at least two feet of open space around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and a professional tune-up every spring and fall, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

How old is your system? Its age tells you a lot. With regular service, most air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is past that range and is also showing higher bills, uneven temperatures, or repeated repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement in peak season.

The sooner you call about a comfort problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A weak capacitor is an easy, low-cost part to replace, yet that same capacitor, ignored through a full season, can take out the compressor and force a full system replacement. Save our number in your contacts now so help is one call away the moment something seems off in your home. Early intervention saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
